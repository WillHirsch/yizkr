# Current project contract

[REQUIREMENTS](REQUIREMENTS.md) defines the user outcomes. This document defines cross-cutting policy; [MODELING](MODELING.md) records implemented semantics, and focused architecture guides and runbooks describe supported operations. Read the relevant documents for a change, not the entire ADR collection. A policy stated here is not evidence that its implementation exists. [The ingestion and identity contract](INGESTION_CONTRACT.md) specifies source ownership, durable assignments, scoped approvals and shared stage boundaries for the implementing tasks.

## Evidence, interpretations and correction

Historical assertions carry source provenance and the most reliable available location. Machine interpretations also identify their process. Derived conclusions identify their inputs and method; personal attestations identify their curator and context. Schema and run bookkeeping need appropriate origin and versioning, not invented citations to historical artifacts.

Preserve genuine source disagreement, including within one source. Machine alternatives may remain after review as labeled interpretations; they are not independent witnesses. The presence of an assertion does not establish its truth, its subject's identity or permission to disclose it. Views must work without a canonical value and make disagreement visible, while allowing honest summaries with access to attributed details.

The target correction contract permits audited correction, retraction or supersession of machine errors, duplicates and misattribution while preserving evidence and recoverable history. It does not make every erroneous machine statement permanently authoritative. Existing retained-state correction, identity and disclosure guards remain until supported replacements are verified: [the ingestion contract](INGESTION_CONTRACT.md), [#142](https://github.com/WillHirsch/yizkr/issues/142). This policy authorizes no destructive operation on a retained graph.

## Human authority and privacy

Retained writes require bounded, inspectable approval. Identity assignments and changes, merges, splits and face associations require affirmative confirmation of what is proposed. A batch may confirm several explicitly shown decisions; a general run approval cannot silently confirm faces. One recorded act approves the rows of one stored, immutable sheet, and a sheet is bounded by content: one source's claims, or one cohort's identity proposals. A row always needs its own marked decision when the proposal itself is risky: an identity proposal from an ambiguous or conflicting cluster, a proposal that conflicts with a stored human decision or is blocked, or a claim with a warning that policy classes as blocking. Every row the act covers gets its own decision record, marked as made by that act and citing the sheet, so an approval still binds one proposal's meaning. The rule depends on what is approved, never on which run or increment it is in, and face associations keep their own explicit confirmation. Today a decision exists only where a reviewer marks a row; the act is the target of [#905](https://github.com/WillHirsch/yizkr/issues/905) for identity sheets and [#907](https://github.com/WillHirsch/yizkr/issues/907) for claim sheets. Preserve approvals for unchanged results. New corroborating evidence alone does not invalidate an approval; material changes to content, attribution, identity assignment or its basis require scoped review. [The ingestion contract](INGESTION_CONTRACT.md#decisions-and-approval-carry-forward) defines the change and approval boundaries.

Keep archive data private by default. Publication and access by additional audiences require deliberate authorization. Existing privacy predicates and cutoffs remain until their own approved replacements are implemented; a non-privileged export is not a public-safe archive. Privacy decisions in [#70](https://github.com/WillHirsch/yizkr/issues/70) and [#72](https://github.com/WillHirsch/yizkr/issues/72) do not block unrelated cleanup. The [agent data boundary](AGENT_PROTOCOL.md#6-the-data-boundary) governs development access.

## Rebuild and retained operation

Disposable rebuilds and retained-database ingestion are permanent modes, and both write historical claims through one writer. The assignment writer, which applies reviewed claims to accepted mention assignments and records their support receipts, is the only writer of historical claims on a retained instance, from the first increment on. Its unreviewed mode is permitted only on a designated disposable instance; it records no human decision and cannot make an instance retained. Scratch runs therefore exercise the write path that retained data uses, and no retained map-written graph exists to migrate. A scratch run may write unattended, unratified development proposals to an explicitly disposable instance. It must not write face/depicts associations, authorize disclosure or make the database retained implicitly. Every reviewed write to a retained instance is an increment under one set of rules, and an increment may hold any number of sources, the whole corpus included. The first increment differs only in that the graph is empty: this contract defines nothing for a first retained dataset or a keeper run that it does not define for every increment. A scratch run is the same procedure under the unreviewed policy on a designated disposable instance. Vocabulary bootstrap, establishing the deployment generation and designating the instance retained are instance setup that happens once, not write rules. Retained ingestion must preserve persistent identities, human edits and approvals, and refuse destructive reset. Reset is therefore no remedy once an instance is designated retained, and identity decisions of an earlier increment are fixed when later sources arrive, so results can depend on the order of increments. As policy, the place pass writes no place-containment link to a retained instance for now ([#874](https://github.com/WillHirsch/yizkr/issues/874) is to make the present place writer refuse one); the target, in which container names are mentions and each link is a reviewed claim, is [#929](https://github.com/WillHirsch/yizkr/issues/929). A containment that a document's own `part_of` claim gives is not affected by that decision: it is reviewed and applied like any other claim. As policy, nothing writes a confirmed face-region `depicts` until faces work resumes ([#160](https://github.com/WillHirsch/yizkr/issues/160)), and no route confirms or writes one. The one writer and the increment procedure are the target of [#822](https://github.com/WillHirsch/yizkr/issues/822), with [#876](https://github.com/WillHirsch/yizkr/issues/876) for many sources and [#869](https://github.com/WillHirsch/yizkr/issues/869) for commands; the next paragraph gives what is implemented.

[Durable identity review](IDENTITY_REVIEW.md) records scoped human decisions independently of caches/maps. Shared assignment, provenance and support-receipt APIs implement bounded reviewed additions; the [one-source workflow](SOURCE_INGESTION.md) connects them to durable document products and separate identity/claim review. The whole-corpus driver and legacy map-bound writers retain their existing guards, which stay in force. As implemented, the scratch write stage writes through the map-bound `BatchWriter.write_batch`, and the assignment writer serves one plain-text source, or a run's saved views of a standalone image, through a Python API. The target is that the map-bound writer writes no retained data and is retired once scratch runs use the assignment writer ([#822](https://github.com/WillHirsch/yizkr/issues/822)). The whole-corpus identity map supplies proposals to durable identity review and is not the authority at write time. Today the map-bound writer resolves mentions through it, and `cluster_proposals` turns its result into review proposals ([proposals from clustering](IDENTITY_REVIEW.md#proposals-from-clustering)). Reviewed correction and split remain under [#142](https://github.com/WillHirsch/yizkr/issues/142); this narrow additions path is not yet the whole increment procedure.

Before an instance is designated retained and takes its first increment, workflows must be safe and supported, with bounded review, retries, correction and recovery, privacy and backups, and the assignment path must handle the whole corpus: its scale, every source type, an operational entry point and the unreviewed mode ([#822](https://github.com/WillHirsch/yizkr/issues/822)). This does not require a fixed number of unchanged extraction runs. An instance is designated retained while it is still empty, holding only its bootstrapped vocabulary and deployment generation. From then on reset refuses, and a failed first increment is recovered like any other. A rehearsal of the first increment in reviewed mode on a disposable instance comes first. The designation is the target of [#904](https://github.com/WillHirsch/yizkr/issues/904); implemented today are the kept-state markers and the reset refusal that [ADR-0025](adr/0025-development-by-scratch-runs.md#6-readiness-for-retained-state) describes. Only the owner's authorization in [#159](https://github.com/WillHirsch/yizkr/issues/159) designates an instance retained.

The [isolated Wikibase/WDQS integration lane](TESTING.md#live-integration-and-the-transition) runs in CI as `fictional-integration` and through `make test-integration`. It uses repository-fictional fixtures, separate settings, volumes, state and locks, and fails when setup is unavailable or the required boundary checks do not run successfully. It creates and removes its own stacks without archive credentials, mounts or implicit archive defaults. A successful demonstration with the tested revision, commands and real checks must be recorded before [#159](https://github.com/WillHirsch/yizkr/issues/159) retains the shared instance, and so must the rehearsal of the first increment in reviewed mode on a disposable instance ([#904](https://github.com/WillHirsch/yizkr/issues/904)). This lane establishes selected writer/query behavior, not complete retained-workflow readiness.

An isolated fictional deployment may run in a cloud session under the dispatch gate. Corpus-derived graphs remain local-only. Additional checks against an existing deployment require an explicitly designated disposable target and its local lock. Missing integration evidence blocks affected persistence changes; it does not block unrelated documentation or offline work. Never reset a retained instance to run tests.

## Implementation boundaries and freedom

- **Identity:** distinguish source bytes, placements, source parts, readings, mentions and persistent entities. Labels, filenames and cluster snapshots are not durable entity identifiers. [The ingestion contract](INGESTION_CONTRACT.md#source-identity-and-attribution) specifies their identities and durability; [the container inventory](ARCHITECTURE.md#container-inventory) describes common container handling.
- **Grounding:** source reading and extraction remain grounded in the source. Reconciliation and planning may use an explicitly recorded read-only graph snapshot or projection, with relevant write preconditions checked before mutation. The reviewed additions API refreshes relevant graph context under server exclusion before accepting identities; source preparation never uses graph facts as historical evidence.
- **Reuse:** persisted document products, review and decisions must remain usable without the model-response cache. A response key represents the actual inputs and configuration; conversation reuse must account for its opening answer and context. Session ownership and cancellation are explicit. [Execution scopes](ARCHITECTURE.md#cancellation-and-execution-ownership) own cancellation; an [execution-owned session service](ARCHITECTURE.md#provider-conversation-ownership) manages restoration, replay and release separately from response storage. The cache retains its exclusive process-lifetime lock.
- **Platform:** Wikibase and the Python package remain the current implementation. Maintain one authority for accepted assertions; this does not require all media, operational metadata or proposals to live in the graph. No platform replacement or general-purpose framework is required by this contract.

## Proportionate verification

Choose evidence for the changed behavior and plausible failure modes. Use focused tests while iterating and broad offline CI for the reviewed revision. Repeat or broaden local testing when failures or unresolved risk justify it, not automatically after every push. Documentation and mechanical changes need relevant formatting, link and consistency checks; Markdown prompts and queries are behavior resources, so assess their actual effects.

Keep meaningful safeguards. For refactors, exercise affected existing callers and tests; add cases for actual coverage gaps. Parser and schema changes need targeted valid and rejected fictional cases. Write and query changes need checks at the real boundary. Privacy, exclusions and review gates need positive and negative cases at their enforcement boundary. Concurrency checks should exercise controlled threads or processes and locks; scheduling may use a fake clock. Performance claims need measurements, not merely a generous timeout. CI has a [time budget from push to a complete result](TESTING.md#lane-time-budget); a change whose rerun is still over it is not ready until it is back within it or the owner has chosen a higher stack limit, more runners or a new figure.

Mutation testing is selective and optional. A valid negative control or reproduced failure can demonstrate that a regression test discriminates; a setup failure cannot. New test cases do not each require a mutation table.

The [small deterministic semantic acceptance set](TESTING.md#semantic-acceptance) complements corpus evidence; it validates selected contracts without real-model evaluation. Canned model responses demonstrate wiring, not extraction quality. Material prompt, model or inference changes need schema/wiring checks and observed outputs from a bounded representative sample or suitable existing corpus evidence, with configuration recorded. A purely mechanical edit does not automatically require a model call.

Use whole-corpus evidence where corpus interactions, material uncertainty, unexplained regressions or the readiness of an instance to be designated retained warrant it. Apply `needs:scratch-diff` with a reason and the relevant `stage:*` label; it is not automatic for every change before write. Reuse compatible responses and run only necessary downstream work; stop before writes when graph effects are irrelevant. Changes merge in the order of the corpus evidence they need, and whole-corpus evidence is read after they merge, when the affected stage is next run on main, by a run that covers every change merged since its baseline; a regression it shows is fixed by a follow-up change. A change to the clustering settings is the exception, with its scratch diff and the owner's sign-off before merge ([AGENT_PROTOCOL §5](AGENT_PROTOCOL.md#5-integration-and-corpus-evidence)). Remaining brittle-test migration belongs to the affected subsystem changes under [the testing guidance](TESTING.md#ownership-of-test-migration), not as a prerequisite for unrelated development. [TESTING](TESTING.md) supplies commands, current limitations and the separate optional-engine compatibility check.

## Current-state documentation

Keep the repository's current tree limited to current goals, contract, supported behavior, limitations and necessary present-tense rationale. Development chronology, abandoned designs, amendment logs and supersession history belong in Git, PRs and issues. Update, merge or delete existing guidance; do not require a new ADR for every architecture change or retain tombstones or an in-repository archive.

Supported compatibility and migration instructions remain current documentation while needed. Temporarily retained deprecated material must be clearly non-authoritative for new policy and carry a nearby removal TODO linked to an open issue with a concrete removal condition. One coherent task can cover multiple passages; the work remains outstanding until the material is removed.

Source evidence, curator audit history and meaningful regression inputs are current data with continuing purposes; this policy does not authorize deleting them. Approved target requirements must be distinguished from implemented capabilities with a limitation and implementation task.

Revisit a requirement when representative use cases or operational evidence reveal a mismatch. Record a provisional default and a concrete follow-up for unresolved questions, and block only work whose imminent decisions depend on them. The issue/branch/draft-PR/review/owner-merge workflow remains in [AGENT_PROTOCOL](AGENT_PROTOCOL.md); no additional universal planning gate is implied.

- What a document is: these properties state who made a document, for whom, when, where, what it is about, whom it shows and who issued it. Their subject is THIS document, given exactly as the source's artifact: {{"kind": "artifact", "sha1": "{artifact_sha1}"}}; an image this document places, by its artifact reference exactly as listed below; or a document this text mentions (a letter's enclosure), by its local reference, an `unclassified_entity` whose type_hint (in the entity manifest) or label says what it is ("death certificate", "photograph"); and never anything else. Never give an entity an artifact kind (`photograph`, `letter`, `certificate` and the like): a document the text mentions is an `unclassified_entity` with a type_hint.
  - `issued_by`: the organization that issued it, where the source shows it issuing (the register office of a certificate, the court of a judgment);
  - `author`: who wrote, drew or made it, where it names them (a letter's signature, a studio's name on a photograph's mount); a signature that only certifies or registers (a registrar's, a notary's) is no authorship;
  - `addressee`: whom it is addressed to (a letter's greeting, an envelope's address);
  - `inception`: when it was made (a letter's dateline), at the precision it gives;
  - `location_of_creation`: where it was made (a letter's heading);
  - `main_subject`: what it is about (a letter's topic, the district a map covers);
  - `shows`: whom or what an image shows, as its content or the passage names them (an inscription on a print: one claim per name, no position read into their order); never `depicts`, which only the faces flow gives.

  About THIS document, give each only where the document states it of itself, never from what another document says of it. About an image it places or a document it mentions, give one only where a passage of THIS document, read where it stands, conveys that fact of that image or document, with that passage as the claim's transcription excerpt and its page number the page the passage stands on: a claim about another document always carries its excerpt. A letter that says it encloses a man's death certificate conveys what the certificate is about: its `main_subject` is that man. No other property takes a document as its subject.
